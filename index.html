Nº 1Personal Finance in Claude

Your money, read‑only.

Claude can now look at your bank, card, loan and investment accounts through Plaid, and answer questions about them. It can look. It can't move a cent.

Beta · United States · Pro, Max and Ultra plans · announced 5 November 2026

The water in this deck is live. Stir it with your cursor or a finger: it moves, and it never leaves the glass.

Nº 2What it is

Four kinds of account, one page to ask about them.

Personal Finance is a beta inside Claude. You link the accounts you choose. Claude shows balances and spending on one page and answers questions from your own transactions.

  • The page is made of widgets: spending, cash flow, accounts, subscriptions, upcoming bills and short insights. You can add, edit, remove and reorder them.
  • Ask on that page or in a regular chat: where last month's money went, which subscriptions you pay for, what's due before payday.
  • Anthropic's own examples go further: compare a month with earlier ones, spot duplicate charges and fees, explain APR and total the interest, build charts and bill calendars.

Sources: Anthropic, “Introducing Personal Finance in Claude”; Claude Help Center; release notes, 5 Nov 2026.

Nº 3What Claude can see

What it reads, and what stays dark.

Reads, for shared accounts only

  • bank name; account name, type and last four digits
  • balances and credit limits
  • up to 24 months of transactions: amount, date, merchant, category, the bank's description

Doesn't read

  • your bank password: you sign in at Plaid, never in Claude
  • investment holdings or trades
  • interest rates or loan terms
  • accounts you didn't share

Two gotchas. Plaid's screens may ask consent for investment, credit or loan data, but Claude currently reads only the list above. And a full account number can still slip in if your bank puts it in an account name or a transaction description.

Source: Claude Help Center, “Use Personal Finance in Claude”.

Nº 4The one rule

It can look. It can't touch.

“Claude's access to connected accounts is read-only, so it can't move money.”Plaid, announcement post, 5 Nov 2026

Anthropic says the same: Personal Finance can't move, manage or invest your money. No transfer, no payment, no trade.

Nº 5Under the hood

The path your data takes.

  1. You sign in at Plaid. Plaid is a service that links apps to banks. Claude never asks for or sees your bank password.
  2. You consent at Plaid. You approve read-only access and pick which accounts to share.
  3. Plaid passes figures on. It shares what the feature needs, organizes the raw transaction data and refreshes it when needed.
  4. Claude answers. The data is used to respond to your requests. Hosting providers handle it too, and Plaid handles it under its own terms and privacy policy.

Expect a lag: balances can be a day or two old and may differ from your bank's.

Sources: Help Center; Plaid blog; Anthropic announcement.

Nº 6How to link

Linking, in seven steps.

  1. Open Personal Finance in the side menu. Not there? Hover over More.
  2. Select Get started.
  3. Choose your goals, or skip them, then Continue.
  4. Select Link accounts with Plaid.
  5. On Connect Plaid to Claude, review the access, then Continue.
  6. In Plaid: choose your bank, sign in, choose the accounts to share.
  7. Back in Claude, wait for the accounts to appear, then Link another account or Done.

Finish Plaid within 10 minutes and come straight back. Transactions can take a few minutes to sync.

Source: Help Center, whose ten small steps are grouped into seven here. Button names quoted from it.

Nº 7More banks, and the awkward cases

Up to 16 banks.

  • Add one with Link another account: top of the page on web and Desktop, behind the gear icon on iOS and Android. At 16 you must unlink one first.
  • New account at a bank you already linked? Link that bank again and share every account you want included.
  • If Claude says the bank is already linked: Switch to the new link replaces the old sign-in, Keep it as it is links nothing new, Keep both is for a second login such as a joint or business one.
  • With Keep both, totals can double-count an account the bank shows two ways.

Nº 8How to unlink

Closing the window.

  1. In Personal Finance, open your linked accounts: … then Manage linked accounts on web and Desktop, the gear icon on mobile.
  2. Select Unlink on the bank (on mobile, tap the bank first), then Unlink again to confirm.

Claude stops reading every account under that bank login; you can't unlink a single account. Your money doesn't go anywhere. Past chats, artifacts and shared links keep what they said until you delete them.

3 banks linked. The dashboard is intact.

To stop entirely: Unlink all banks, or disconnect Claude at my.plaid.com. If a bank ends access itself, Sign in again restores it and the dashboard stays as it was.

Source: Help Center.

Nº 9Privacy, memory and training

What happens to the conversation.

  • Chats in the Personal Finance hub aren't used to train Anthropic's models. Outside the hub, your usual privacy settings apply.
  • Chats that used your accounts are designed not to be saved to memory, can't be searched later and are left out of your monthly recap, title included.
  • Your data stays in chat history until you delete the chat, subject to Anthropic's retention practices.
  • Claude asks before publishing an artifact from such a chat. The artifact can hold bank data and outlives the chat. A shared chat link shows the account figures in it.
  • An artifact can read the linked accounts of whoever opens it, if they have Personal Finance and allow Plaid.

Sources: Help Center; Anthropic announcement.

Nº 10Controls

Who opens the tap.

By default Claude reads your linked accounts in a chat without asking each time. It always asks before linking or unlinking a bank in a chat.

  • Web and Desktop: click + in the message box, then Connectors, and switch Plaid off or on.
  • iOS and Android: tap +, Connectors, Plaid. Under All tools (Android: Set all tools) choose Blocked or Always allow.
  • Needs approval asks before each read, unless the chat is set to Automatically approve. Your choice applies to new chats too.

Source: Help Center. Setting names quoted from it.

Nº 11Availability and cost

Who gets it, where.

  • Beta, United States and US banks only. It has been rolling out gradually since 5 November 2026, so it may not show up for you yet.
  • Pro, Max and Ultra plans, at no stated extra charge. Not Free, Team or Enterprise.
  • Claude on the web, Claude Desktop, and the iOS and Android apps.
  • Travelling, or on a VPN that exits in another country? It hides until you're back. Your banks stay linked.
  • Not in Claude Duet, scheduled tasks, Cowork tasks or Claude Code. It may not work inside projects.

Sources: Help Center; release notes; Anthropic announcement. No change found as of 16 Nov 2026.

Nº 12Limits and gotchas

Read this before you trust a total.

  • Balances can be a day or two old, and figures may differ from your bank's.
  • A bank Claude can't read is left out of spending figures, so a budget can show more money left than you have.
  • In chats that used your accounts Claude shows text and tables and can make plain-text files such as .csv. Running code and Word, Excel, PowerPoint or PDF files may not work.
  • A chat that already ran code, made files or started from an artifact can't use your accounts. Start a new chat outside a project.
  • If your paid plan ends you can't open it, but banks stay linked until you unlink them. Moving to a Team or Enterprise organization unlinks them; so does deleting your account.

Source: Help Center.

Nº 13How Anthropic frames it

A sharp pair of eyes, not an adviser.

Anthropic says Personal Finance helps you understand your money and isn't designed to give investment advice.

  • No ads and no sponsored recommendations.
  • Anthropic sells no financial products of its own: no credit cards, loans or savings accounts.
  • For bigger decisions, Anthropic calls this a first step and says it is exploring how Claude could work alongside licensed professionals and regulated firms.
  • Never share your bank login or full account numbers with anyone, Claude Support included.

Nothing in this deck is financial advice either. Move the lens over the water: it reads, and that's all it does.

Sources: Anthropic announcement; Help Center.

Nº 14First project, part 1 of 4

The monthly spending review.

Link one account, open Personal Finance, and paste this:

Prompt
Total what I spent last month across my linked accounts, grouped by category. Compare each category with the two months before, flag any that rose by more than 20%, and list the transactions behind the three biggest categories.

Then follow up: “Turn that into a weekly spending chart on my dashboard.” Ask which transactions sit behind any figure that looks off.

Nº 15First project, part 2 of 4

The subscription audit.

Prompt
Find every recurring charge in the last 12 months. For each one give the merchant, how often it bills, the latest amount and whether the price has gone up. Flag possible duplicate charges and fees I might not expect, and show the transactions you used.

Claude can't cancel anything: access is read-only. You cancel with the merchant yourself.

Nº 16First project, part 3 of 4

The debt payoff picture.

Claude sees card and loan balances and credit limits, not their rates or terms. Type your rates in.

Prompt
List my card and loan balances and credit limits. My rates are [card: 24.9% APR] and [car loan: 6.4% APR]. Explain what APR means here, estimate this month's interest on each, and show how long each takes to clear if I pay [$400] a month in total, smallest balance first versus highest rate first. Show your math.

Bracketed values are placeholders: swap in your own.

Nº 17First project, part 4 of 4

The net-worth snapshot.

Prompt
Make a net-worth snapshot: add the balances of my checking, savings and investment accounts, subtract my card and loan balances, and show it as a table by account. Say how current each balance is, and point out any account that looks missing or double-counted.

Investments show balances only, not holdings, so this is a top-line number, not a portfolio review. If you used Keep both at one bank, check for double counting.
